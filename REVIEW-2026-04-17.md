# UT1 Blacklist Classification Accuracy Audit

## Executive Summary

We audited 600 domains across three UT1 blacklist categories — `bank`, `bitcoin`, and `gambling` — to assess classification accuracy for use as an AI agent access-control mechanism. Each domain was visited, its content evaluated, and its classification judged against the category definition.

The lists are highly accurate. Of 600 domains reviewed, we identified only 1 entry requiring removal (`ica.se`, a grocery retailer in the bank list), 8 genuine false positives across the other two lists, and a handful of debatable entries. Overall accuracy ranges from 96% to 99.5% depending on category.

| Category | List Size | Sampled | Accuracy | False Positives Found |
|----------|-----------|---------|----------|-----------------------|
| `bank/domains` | 6,645 | 200 (3.0%) | **99.5%** | 1 removed, 3 debatable |
| `bitcoin/domains` | 1,419 | 200 (14.1%) | **96–98%** | 4 unrelated, 4 repurposed |
| `gambling/domains` | 32,242 | 200 (0.6%) | **98%** | 4 false positives, 5 debatable |

The UT1 blacklists, maintained by Fabrice Prigent at Université Toulouse Capitole, contain 2.1M+ domains across 75 categories. This audit focused on three categories relevant to restricting AI agent access to high-impact financial and gambling actions.

---

## 1. Motivation

Large language model agents increasingly operate on the open web — browsing, filling forms, and executing transactions. Without guardrails, an agent could inadvertently (or through prompt injection) initiate wire transfers, place bets, or trade cryptocurrency. Domain-level blocklists offer a simple, transparent, and auditable mechanism to prevent this class of failure.

The UT1 blacklists were not designed for this purpose — they originate from a French university web filtering project — but their category structure maps well to the AI agent safety use case. Before adopting them, we needed to verify that the classifications are accurate enough to avoid both safety gaps (false negatives) and collateral damage (false positives).

---

## 2. Methodology

### 2.1 Sampling

For each of the three categories, we drew 200 domains using a combination of:

- **Evenly-spaced systematic sampling** (every Nth line) for the initial 20 per category, ensuring coverage across the alphabetically sorted list.
- **Random sampling** (`shuf`) for the remaining 180 per category, with deduplication against previously reviewed entries.
- **Targeted sampling** for the bank list, using keyword filters to ensure representation of US and Swedish markets specifically.

The 200-domain sample represents 3.0% of the bank list, 14.1% of the bitcoin list, and 0.6% of the gambling list.

### 2.2 Evaluation

Each domain was visited via HTTPS using automated web fetching. For each domain, we recorded:

1. **HTTP status** — whether the site was reachable (200, redirect) or not (403, 503, DNS NXDOMAIN).
2. **Actual content** — what the site serves today.
3. **Classification judgment** — YES (agrees with category), NO (disagrees), or UNCERTAIN.

When a site was unreachable, we supplemented with:
- Web search for the domain name
- Historical context (known brands, archived content)
- Domain name semantics (e.g., `solcasino-north.top` is unambiguously gambling)

### 2.3 Classification Criteria

We adopted a broad interpretation of each category, aligned with the AI agent safety use case:

- **Bank**: Any site where financial transactions can be initiated or facilitated — banks, credit unions, payment processors, merchant services, loan brokers, and financial comparison platforms. Trade associations and financial news are debatable.
- **Bitcoin**: Any site related to cryptocurrency operations — exchanges, wallets, mining pools, faucets, DeFi platforms, blockchain explorers, crypto news, and token projects.
- **Gambling**: Any site offering or promoting gambling — casinos, sportsbooks, lotteries, poker, bingo, slot sites, and gambling affiliate/review sites.

Parked domains, hijacked domains, and defunct-but-historically-correct entries were all counted as acceptable — blocking a dead domain causes no collateral damage, and the domain could be re-registered for its original purpose.

### 2.4 Execution

The review was parallelized across 24 independent agents, each responsible for a batch of 20–30 domains. Agents operated concurrently, visiting sites and reporting structured findings. Results were compiled, deduplicated, and reconciled into this report.

## 3. Results

### 3.1 Bank List — 6,645 Domains

**Accuracy: 99.5%** (1 removal in 200 samples)

The bank list is the highest-quality of the three. It contains a comprehensive set of financial institutions worldwide, with strong coverage of:

- US community banks and federal credit unions (hundreds of entries)
- Major global banks (Bank of America, Citibank, HSBC, ING, Nordea, etc.)
- Swedish financial institutions (all "Big 4" — Handelsbanken, SEB, Nordea, Swedbank — plus niche players like JAK, Marginalen, FOREX Bank)
- Payment processors (Redecard, FTPS/Worldpay)
- International banks across Europe, Asia, Latin America, and Africa

#### Removed

| Domain | Issue |
|--------|-------|
| `ica.se` | ICA is Sweden's largest grocery retailer. While it operates a banking subsidiary (ICA Banken at `icabanken.se`), the `ica.se` domain is the grocery shopping portal — recipes, meal kits, food delivery. Blocking it prevents grocery access, not banking. |

#### Debatable — Trade Associations

| Domain | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| `cbak.com` | Community Bankers Association of Kansas |
| `ibcbanks.org` | Independent Community Bankers of Colorado |
| `aseanbankers.org` | ASEAN Bankers Association |

These are banking industry advocacy organizations. They don't offer financial services, but an AI agent has no legitimate reason to interact with them either. Low-priority decision.

#### Observations

- **Parked/for-sale domains** (5 of 200, 2.5%): Former bank domains now listed on domain marketplaces. Harmless to keep.
- **Defunct banks** (~10 of 200, 5%): Merged, acquired, or closed institutions. Many still redirect to successor banks (e.g., `opusbank.com` → Columbia Bank, `nationalcity.com` → PNC). Correct to keep.
- **`.bank` TLD migration**: A notable trend — US banks are migrating to the verified `.bank` TLD (`pilgrimbank.bank`, `visionbank.bank`, `nve.bank`). The old `.com` domains still redirect, but the list should consider tracking `.bank` domains as well.
- **One hijacked domain**: `fricktricountyfcu.org` now serves a Vavada online casino. The legitimate credit union rebranded to `frickfin.org`. This is the only entry that could cause harm if the list were used as an allowlist.

### 3.2 Bitcoin List — 1,419 Domains

**Accuracy: 96–98%** (4–8 issues in 200 samples)

The bitcoin list provides broad coverage of the cryptocurrency ecosystem: major exchanges (Bitstamp, Huobi/HTX, KuCoin, OKX, OKCoin), wallets (Trust Wallet, Electrum, Tonkeeper, WalletConnect), mining pools (2miners, ViaBTC, Ocean, public-pool.io), DeFi platforms (PancakeSwap), blockchain infrastructure (Infura, Helius, NowNodes), faucets (freebitco.in), and mining software (HiveOS, MinerGate, ccminer).

#### Genuine False Positives

| Domain | What It Actually Is |
|--------|-------------------|
| `geetle.com` | Generic inactive website with a contact form. No crypto connection. |
| `gus.host` | Personal redirect to `snek.dev` (a developer's homepage). |
| `dyqqq.com` | Parked domain for sale. Name has no crypto association. |
| `tutor-web.info` | University of Iceland educational platform. Used SmileyCoin as a minor student reward mechanism, but the site's purpose was teaching, not cryptocurrency. |

#### Repurposed Domains

These were historically crypto-related but now serve entirely different content:

| Domain | Original Purpose | Current Content |
|--------|-----------------|-----------------|
| `butkoin.com` | Crypto-sounding domain | Indonesian gambling portal |
| `butterflylabs.com` | Bitcoin ASIC manufacturer (shut down by FTC) | Lifestyle magazine blog |
| `cryptobara.com` | Cryptocurrency site | Survey/rewards redirect |
| `lightninginabox.co` | Bitcoin Lightning Network hardware vendor | Casino game promotional page |

#### Borderline

| Domain | Issue |
|--------|-------|
| `aipowergrid.io` | Decentralized AI compute platform with a blockchain token. Core purpose is AI inference, not crypto. |
| `elitetrader.io` | Browser fingerprinting tracker/redirect page. No substantive content. |
| `epargne-crypto.io` | Same tracker pattern. "Crypto savings" in French but no real site. |
| `myregeneaf.com` | Completely dead — no DNS, no archive, no search results. Unverifiable. |

#### Observations

- **High domain rot** (~40–50% unreachable): Mining pools and altcoin projects have notoriously short lifespans. Many sampled domains return 503, 403, or DNS NXDOMAIN. This is expected and not a quality problem — the entries were correct when added.
- **Cryptojacking domains**: The list includes known cryptojacking script hosts (`anomp.cc`, `azvjudwr.info`, `jroqvbvw.info`, `authedmine.com`). These are correctly categorized — they delivered in-browser cryptocurrency miners.
- **IP addresses**: The list contains bare IPs (e.g., `69.62.247.146`, `84.90.165.217`) which are likely mining pool stratum endpoints. These are harder to verify but consistent with the list's purpose.
- **Overlap with `cryptojacking` category**: UT1 maintains a separate `cryptojacking` list (16,289 domains). Some entries in `bitcoin` could also fit there. The categories are complementary, not redundant.

### 3.3 Gambling List — 32,242 Domains

**Accuracy: 98%** (4 false positives in 200 samples)

The gambling list is the largest of the three by an order of magnitude. It is dominated by disposable casino mirror domains — short-lived clones of grey-market online casinos that rotate across cheap TLDs to evade blocks and search engine penalties.

#### Genuine False Positives

| Domain | What It Actually Is | Why It's Wrong |
|--------|-------------------|----------------|
| `mkgamble.com` | Law Office MK Gamble, LLC | "Gamble" is the attorney's surname. The site offers estate planning, real estate, and business law services. |
| `casinopawnbrokers.com.au` | Pawnshop in Brisbane, Australia | "Casino" is the business's trade name. The site deals in gold, jewellery, and collateral loans — not gambling. |
| `supercasino.axome.com` | French web agency (Axome, Shopify Platinum Partner) | Subdomain redirects to `axome.com`. Likely an old client project reference. |
| `pub-79b7fbba87c04f4489bc2e0dd3bfd753.r2.dev` | Cloudflare R2 storage bucket | Generic CDN URL returning 404. No inherent gambling association. Likely hosted gambling assets temporarily. |

#### Debatable

| Domain | Issue |
|--------|-------|
| `mgmresortsimages.com` | MGM Resorts image CDN — infrastructure, not a gambling site |
| `mgmmiragevoice.com` | MGM corporate communications domain |
| `okcasinocareers.com` | Casino employment/careers site |
| `mrgmail.com` | Dead domain, name resembles gmail.com typosquat |
| `dayofshow.com` | Unreachable; name suggests events/entertainment |

#### Observations

- **Extreme domain churn**: ~60–70% of sampled domains are now dead (DNS NXDOMAIN). Gambling mirror operators register domains in bulk on cheap TLDs (`.top`, `.buzz`, `.win`, `.xyz`, `.club`, `.lol`), use them briefly, and abandon them. The list captures this churn but accumulates dead weight rapidly.
- **Keyword matching limitations**: The `mkgamble.com` and `casinopawnbrokers.com.au` false positives demonstrate that keyword matching on "gamble" and "casino" produces occasional false positives when these words appear in unrelated business names.
- **Valuable non-obvious catches**: `cartoonfile.net` (hiding an Indonesian slot gambling site behind an innocuous name) and `casino-lasvegas-live.com` (redirecting to `thetoycabin.com`, another disguised gambling portal) show the list's value beyond simple keyword matching — it captures domains that would evade naive text filters.
- **State lotteries included**: `illinoislottery.com` (official Illinois State Lottery) is correctly categorized as gambling.

## 4. Cross-Category Insights

### 4.1 Domain Lifecycle Patterns

Each category exhibits a distinct domain lifecycle:

| Category | Typical Domain Lifespan | % Dead in Sample | Primary Cause of Death |
|----------|------------------------|-------------------|----------------------|
| Bank | 10+ years | ~5% | Mergers/acquisitions; redirects to successor |
| Bitcoin | 1–5 years | ~40–50% | Project abandonment, exchange collapse, pool shutdown |
| Gambling | Weeks to months | ~60–70% | Intentional rotation to evade blocks |

This has implications for list maintenance: the bank list is relatively stable, the bitcoin list needs periodic pruning, and the gambling list grows fastest but also has the highest dead-domain ratio.

### 4.2 False Positive Patterns

| Pattern | Example | Category Affected |
|---------|---------|-------------------|
| **Keyword collision** | `mkgamble.com` (law firm), `casinopawnbrokers.com.au` (pawnshop) | Gambling |
| **Parent company over-blocking** | `ica.se` (grocery chain with bank subsidiary) | Bank |
| **Infrastructure over-blocking** | `mgmresortsimages.com` (CDN), `pub-...r2.dev` (storage) | Gambling |
| **Domain repurposing** | `butterflylabs.com` (now a blog), `butkoin.com` (now gambling) | Bitcoin |
| **Subdomain over-blocking** | `supercasino.axome.com` (web agency client subdomain) | Gambling |

## 5. Limitations

- **Sample size**: 200 of 6,645 (bank), 200 of 1,419 (bitcoin), and 200 of 32,242 (gambling). The gambling sample in particular covers only 0.6% of the list. Rare false positive patterns may be underrepresented.
- **Point-in-time evaluation**: Domains change hands. A domain that is correctly classified today may be repurposed tomorrow, and vice versa. This audit reflects the state of the web as of April 2026.
- **Reachability bias**: ~30–40% of domains were unreachable and evaluated by name/context only. Some classifications may differ if the site were operational.
- **No false negative testing**: We only checked whether listed domains belong in their category. We did not test whether important domains are *missing* from the lists (e.g., whether all major crypto exchanges are covered).
- **Single-category assignment**: UT1 assigns each domain to one category. Some domains span multiple categories (e.g., `bustdice.com` is both bitcoin and gambling; `freebitco.in` is a Bitcoin faucet with gambling mechanics). This is a structural limitation of the list format.

---

## 6. Recommendations

### Immediate (completed or low-effort)

1. **Remove `ica.se` from `bank/domains`** — Done.
2. **Consider adding `icabanken.se` to `bank/domains`** — The actual banking domain for ICA's financial subsidiary.
3. **Remove clear false positives from `gambling/domains`**: `mkgamble.com`, `casinopawnbrokers.com.au`.
4. **Remove clear false positives from `bitcoin/domains`**: `geetle.com`, `gus.host`.

### Medium-term

5. **Track `.bank` TLD adoption** — As US banks migrate to `.bank`, the list should include both old and new domains.
6. **Periodic repurposed-domain sweep for `bitcoin/domains`** — Check domains with non-crypto-related names (no "coin", "mine", "pool", "crypto", "btc" in the hostname) to catch repurposed entries.
7. **Investigate subdomain entries in `gambling/domains`** — Entries like `supercasino.axome.com` and `pub-...r2.dev` suggest that some additions target ephemeral CDN-hosted gambling content. These go stale quickly and risk over-blocking.

### Future Work

8. **False negative analysis** — Audit coverage of known major platforms. Are all top-50 crypto exchanges listed? All FDIC-insured US banks? All major online gambling operators?
9. **Cross-category consistency check** — Identify domains that appear in one category but may better fit another (e.g., crypto-gambling sites in `bitcoin` vs. `gambling`).
10. **Automated freshness monitoring** — Periodically resolve all domains and flag those that have changed content category (e.g., a former bank domain now serving gambling content).
11. **Expand to additional categories** — If using these lists for AI agent safety, categories like `financial` (473 domains), `cryptojacking` (16,289 domains), and `shopping` may also be relevant.
